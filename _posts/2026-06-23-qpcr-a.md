# qPCR Gene Selection: Heat Stress Response in Zebrafish

## Research Question

**At what temperature do zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) begin to exhibit a molecular heat-stress response?**

Zebrafish are tropical freshwater fish native to South Asia. They naturally experience temperature fluctuations in rivers and streams and can tolerate a relatively broad temperature range compared to many fish species. Because of this tolerance, it is not always clear at which temperature zebrafish begin to experience physiological stress.

To investigate this question, I propose exposing zebrafish to increasing water temperatures and measuring the expression of stress-related genes using qPCR. The goal is to identify the temperature at which a molecular stress response becomes detectable.

---

## Experimental Design

Fish will be maintained under the following temperature conditions:

| Group | Temperature (°C) |
|---------|---------|
| Control | 28 |
| Treatment 1 | 32 |
| Treatment 2 | 35 |
| Treatment 3 | 38 |

After exposure, RNA will be extracted and converted to cDNA. Gene expression will then be measured using qPCR.

---

# Target Gene 1: hsp70

## Why did I choose this gene?

The **hsp70** gene encodes Heat Shock Protein 70, one of the most widely studied molecular markers of heat stress.

## Biological Function

HSP70 acts as a molecular chaperone that helps proteins maintain their correct structure during stressful conditions. It prevents protein misfolding and assists in protein repair.

## Expected Change in Expression

I expect **hsp70 expression to increase as temperature increases**.

Expression should remain relatively low at normal temperatures but increase significantly once the fish begin experiencing heat stress.

## Why is it relevant?

Heat shock proteins are among the first genes activated during thermal stress. Therefore, hsp70 is an excellent indicator of when zebrafish begin to perceive elevated temperature as stressful.

---

# Target Gene 2: sod1

## Why did I choose this gene?

The **sod1** gene encodes Superoxide Dismutase 1, an important antioxidant enzyme.

## Biological Function

SOD1 protects cells from oxidative damage by converting reactive oxygen species (ROS) into less harmful molecules.

## Expected Change in Expression

I expect **sod1 expression to increase at higher temperatures**.

Elevated temperatures can increase metabolic activity and oxidative stress, leading to greater production of reactive oxygen species.

## Why is it relevant?

If heat exposure causes oxidative stress in zebrafish, sod1 expression should increase as part of the cellular defense response.

---

# Reference Gene 1: ef1α

## Why did I choose this gene?

The **ef1α** gene is commonly used as a housekeeping gene in fish qPCR studies.

## Biological Function

EF1α is involved in protein synthesis and cellular maintenance.

## Why should it remain stable?

Because it performs a fundamental cellular function, its expression is expected to remain relatively constant across different temperature treatments.

---

# Reference Gene 2: β-actin

## Why did I choose this gene?

The **β-actin** gene is another commonly used housekeeping gene.

## Biological Function

β-actin is a structural protein that is part of the cytoskeleton.

## Why should it remain stable?

β-actin is essential for basic cellular survival. It codes for a highly conserved cytoskeletal protein that maintains cell shape, aids in cell motility, and regulates gene expression. Almost all eukaryotic cells require these functions.

---

# Hypothesis

I hypothesize that zebrafish will tolerate moderate increases in temperature without showing a strong molecular stress response. However, at sufficiently high temperatures, expression of **hsp70** and **sod1** will increase significantly.

Specifically:

- hsp70 expression will increase sharply once heat stress is detected.
- sod1 expression will increase due to elevated oxidative stress.
- ef1α and β-actin expression will remain stable across all temperature treatments.

The results will help identify the temperature threshold at which zebrafish begin to activate molecular stress-response pathways.
